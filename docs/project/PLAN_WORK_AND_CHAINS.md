# Every building, the work it does, and what the village gets out of it

*"Lag alle bygningene og hva slags arbeid de gjør og hva man får ut av det, å
lag en satisfying rekke på slik. En for mat. En for tools. En for våpen og
armor. Og andre ting som du kommer på."*
*"Ta veldig inspirasjon fra minecolonies der. Synes dems er bra."*
— owner, 2026-08-25.

## What MineColonies actually gets right

Worth naming precisely, because "be like MineColonies" is not a design.

1. **Every crafter owns a MATERIAL DOMAIN, not a recipe list.** The Sawmill
   makes anything whose ingredients are only planks, logs or sticks. The
   Stonemason owns the whole stone family. The Baker owns wheat, sugar and
   cocoa; the Chef owns every other food. You never wonder who makes a thing —
   you look at what it is made of.
2. **The chain assembles itself through REQUESTS.** A blacksmith who needs
   iron does not go idle: they post a request, the warehouse fills it if it
   can, a courier carries it, and if nobody has iron the request rises to the
   player. The player wires nothing.
3. **Buildings level up**, and a level buys capacity and reach rather than
   unlocking a shopping list.
4. **Nothing is a dead end.** Every worker's output is somebody else's input,
   or the player's.

What Hearthstead adds on top, and must not lose: **D-007 — every building is
useful alone** (a chain is a multiplier, never a gate), and **chest truth** —
every request is satisfied by items that physically exist and can be stolen.

## The chains

### A — FOOD. From a field to a table people sit at.

```
Farmhouse ─grain──▶ Mill ─flour──▶ Bakery ─bread──┐
    └────vegetables───────────────────────────┐   │
Pasture ─meat, milk─┐                         ▼   ▼
Hunter's Lodge ─game┼──▶ Butcher ─cuts──▶ Kitchen ─meals──▶ Dining Hall
Fishery ─────fish───┘
```

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Farmhouse** | plants, tends and harvests its fields | grain and vegetables — the base of everything |
| **Mill** | grinds grain | flour: the bakery gets far more bread from the same field |
| **Bakery** | bakes | bread — the staple that stops hunger |
| **Pasture** | breeds and tends livestock | meat, milk, wool, leather |
| **Hunter's Lodge** | hunts the wild | game and hides, with no field needed |
| **Fishery** | fishes | fish, from water instead of land |
| **Butcher** | cuts and cures | meat that keeps through winter |
| **Kitchen** | cooks | **meals** — the only food that satisfies the variety need |
| **Dining Hall** | serves | settlers eat *together*: morale and the social need |

**Why it is satisfying:** the first loaf comes fast, and every building after
it makes the same field feed more people. The mill is the first bottleneck a
player has to notice on their own.

### B — TOOLS. Two chains that must meet.

```
Lumber Camp ─logs──▶ Sawmill ─planks, sticks──▶ Carpenter ─hafts────┐
                                    └──furniture──▶ building quality│
                                                                    ▼
Mine ─ore, stone──▶ Smelter ─ingots─────────────────────────▶ Smithy ─▶ TOOLS
```

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Lumber Camp** | fells and replants | logs, and a forest that does not vanish |
| **Sawmill** | saws | planks and sticks, far faster than by hand |
| **Carpenter** | shapes | hafts for tools; furniture that raises every building's quality |
| **Mine** | digs | ore and stone |
| **Smelter** | smelts | ingots, and charcoal |
| **Smithy** | forges and **repairs** | tools that make every other worker faster — and repair, which needs no inputs at all |

**Why it is satisfying:** this is the first chain where two lines meet. A
smithy with metal alone makes rough tools; add the carpenter's hafts and the
same metal makes tools that last three times as long. The player feels the
join.

### C — WEAPONS AND ARMOUR. What the guards carry.

```
Mine ─ore─▶ Smelter ─ingots──┬──▶ Smithy ────▶ swords, axes
                             └──▶ Armoury ───▶ mail, plate
Pasture ─hides─▶ Tannery ─leather──┬──▶ Armoury ───▶ leather armour
Lumber Camp ─sticks─┬──────────────┴──▶ Fletcher ──▶ bows, arrows
Pasture ─feathers───┘
```

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Tannery** | tans hides | leather — the first armour a poor settlement can afford |
| **Fletcher** | fletches | bows and arrows: the only ranged answer to a raid |
| **Armoury** | plates and fits | armour, and the rack the guards actually equip from |

**Why it is satisfying:** it is *visible*. A guard in leather looks different
from a guard in mail, and the raid you survive is the one you kitted out for.
It also gives the mine a second customer, so the smelter stops being a bread
tax.

### D — CLOTH AND WARMTH. Designed, and DEFERRED.

```
Pasture ─wool──▶ Weaver ─cloth──▶ Tailor ──▶ warm clothing
```

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Weaver** | spins and weaves | cloth |
| **Tailor** | cuts and sews | clothing: the warmth need, and settler outfits |

**Why it is satisfying:** the consequence is a season late. A settlement that
ignored the weaver in summer is cold in winter, and no warning is as good as
the first cold night.

**Not being built yet**, by the owner's choice: the whole payoff lands a season
after the work, and seasons do not exist. Shipping it now would mean a chain
whose consequence never arrives. The Weaver's Cottage is in the roster so the
room can exist; the Tailor waits for winter.

### E — DRINK AND PEOPLE. Recruiting has a supply chain.

```
Farmhouse ─barley──┐
Well House ─water──┴──▶ Brewery ─ale──▶ Tavern ──▶ travellers stay, and sign on
```

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Well House** | draws water | water for the brewery and the kitchen, buckets for raid fires |
| **Brewery** | brews | ale |
| **Tavern** | hosts | travellers arrive and can be recruited; settlers relax — morale |

**Why it is satisfying:** population becomes something you *grow*, not
something you wait for.

### F — STONE AND REPAIR. What raids cost, and who pays it.

```
Mine / Quarry ─stone──▶ Mason ─blocks, bricks──▶ repair after a raid
```

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Mason** | dresses stone | blocks and bricks — and the material settlers repair raid damage with |

**Why it is satisfying:** the raid's scar is repaired by a chain the player
built, not by a timer.

### G — KNOWLEDGE. Upstream of everything.

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Architect's Study** | draws | **Build Plans** — nothing else does, so every plaque depends on it |
| **School** | teaches | children grow talents faster; adults retrain |
| **Library** | studies | the active research project (hearth tiers, traditions) |

### H — CARE AND ORDER.

| building | the work | what the village gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Infirmary** | treats | the downed are saved instead of buried |
| **Barracks** | trains | guards, and veterans who rank up |
| **Watchtower** | watches | a raid seen coming, not one that arrives |
| **Warehouse** | keeps | the storage index every request is filled from |
| **Market** | trades | caravans, and the only source of Sølvmark |
| **House / Lodging** | shelters | beds, and the morale that comes from a good one |

## The three mechanisms this needs

Taken from MineColonies, adapted to the invariants here.

1. **Material domains.** Each work building owns a domain — *"anything made
   only of planks, logs or sticks"* is the Sawmill's. A worker can make
   anything in their domain the settlement has the inputs for. This is what
   stops the roster becoming 28 hard-coded recipe lists.
2. **Requests, filled from real chests.** A worker short of iron posts a
   request. The warehouse fills it if it holds iron; a courier carries it —
   visibly, on their back, in the sack A2b already built. If nothing in the
   settlement can fill it, it surfaces to the player in the Tingbok. **No
   request is ever satisfied by items appearing from nowhere.**
3. **Levels buy capacity, not permission.** A level-1 smithy still forges. A
   level-3 smithy forges faster, holds more, and employs two.

## What exists in the code right now

**All 33 building types are in `BuildingType`** — each with its own emblem
item, its requirements built from the vanilla block that station actually is,
and its name in English and Norwegian. A plaque can be dedicated to any of
them today, will survey the room, and will show what is missing.

**No profession works in any of the new ones yet.** That is the next slice, and
the order follows the chains the owner picked: food, then tools, then weapons
and armour, then drink and recruiting, stone and repair, knowledge and care.

The five buildings added for the chains above: **Tannery** (hides → leather),
**Fletcher** (bows and arrows), **Armoury** (armour, and the rack guards equip
from), **Mason's Yard** (dressed stone, and raid repair), **Library** (the
active research project).

## Decided with the owner, 2026-08-25

**Recipes: material domains, no teaching.** Each work building owns a domain —
*"anything made only of planks, logs or sticks"* is the Sawmill's — and its
worker makes whatever in that domain the settlement needs and has inputs for.
This keeps MineColonies' best idea (you always know who makes what) without a
teaching screen, and every building is useful the hour it opens. Teaching was
considered and rejected because a building that does nothing until taught
fights D-007.

**Chains to build:** food, tools, weapons & armour (named by the owner), plus
**drink & recruiting**, **stone & raid repair**, and **knowledge & care**.
Cloth & winter warmth is designed above and **deferred** — its whole payoff is
a season late, and seasons do not exist yet. Building it now would ship a chain
whose consequence never arrives.

**Levels: yes, and they gate professions** — the full MineColonies shape.

### The tension in that last one, and how it resolves

Levels-that-gate is exactly the shape D-007 was written against, and it was
flagged as such when the choice was put. Taking the owner's answer, the two
rules reconcile on one distinction, and the repository should hold this line
rather than two contradictory ones:

> **A building never waits on another BUILDING. It may wait on ITSELF.**

- **D-007 stands, untouched, for cross-building dependencies.** A bakery bakes
  with no mill in the world. A smithy forges with no carpenter. That is what
  "a chain is a multiplier, never a gate" means and it does not bend.
- **Levels gate only what happens INSIDE one building.** Every building at
  level 1 has its primary worker and does its primary job — a level-1 smithy
  forges and repairs. Higher levels admit *additional* professions to the same
  room: the level-3 smithy can also seat an armourer, the level-3 farmhouse a
  second farmhand.

So a level is a decision about where to invest, never a wall between two
buildings. If this still reads as a gate in play — if a player feels stuck
rather than choosing — **D-007 wins and levels lose their gate.**

# Making the flows fit together

*"Heller maximize de flowsa nå. Så kan vi legge til senere — pass på at de
passer utrolig godt sammen."* — owner, 2026-08-25.

So: no more buildings. Six chains are chosen and 33 rooms exist. What follows
is how those six are made to interlock rather than run in parallel lanes —
which is the difference between a settlement and six factories sharing a map.

## The four joins that do the work

A chain that only ever feeds itself is a lane. A chain becomes a *system* at
the point two of them touch. There are four such points, and they are the
design.

### 1. The smelter is contested

```
Mine ─ore──▶ Smelter ─ingots─┬──▶ Smithy   ──▶ tools    (every worker faster)
                             └──▶ Armoury  ──▶ armour   (guards survive)
```

**One smelter cannot feed both at once.** Tools make the whole settlement
productive; armour keeps it alive. When Pressure is rising (A3), the player
must decide whether this week's iron becomes picks or plate — and the decision
is *legible*, because the Tingbok shows both queues.

This is the single most valuable join in the design: it makes the raid system
and the economy argue with each other every week.

### 2. Fuel is one pool

The bakery's ovens, the smelter's forges and the kitchen all burn the same
charcoal, and charcoal comes from the smelter burning the lumber camp's logs.

```
Lumber Camp ─logs─▶ Smelter ─charcoal─┬─▶ Bakery   (bread)
                                      ├─▶ Kitchen  (meals)
                                      └─▶ Smelter  (its own ingots)
```

**A settlement that over-invests in metal goes hungry**, because the same fires
bake the bread. It is a soft limit that never says "no", only "not both, yet".

### 3. The pasture feeds three chains at once

```
              ┌─wool───▶ Weaver      (cloth, later)
Pasture ──────┼─hides──▶ Tannery ──▶ Armoury  (leather armour)
              ├─meat───▶ Butcher ──▶ Kitchen  (meals)
              └─feathers▶ Fletcher            (arrows)
```

One building, four customers. Growing the herd helps food, armour and arrows
together, which makes the pasture the best *second* investment in the game and
a real target for a raider who wants to hurt you everywhere at once.

### 4. Ale is the population valve

```
Farmhouse ─barley─┐
Well House ─water─┴─▶ Brewery ─ale─▶ Tavern ─▶ recruits ─▶ more workers
                                                            │
                                                            └─▶ more mouths
```

**The only loop that feeds back into itself.** More people work more buildings,
which needs more food, which needs more people. The player chooses where on
that curve to sit, and a settlement that recruits faster than it farms starves
— which is a mistake worth being allowed to make.

## The three shared resources

Everything above reduces to three pools that every chain draws on. These are
what the Tingbok should show on one screen, because they are what the player
actually manages:

| pool | fed by | spent by | what running out feels like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Food** | farm, pasture, hunt, fishery → butcher, bakery, kitchen | every settler, every day | morale falls, then people leave |
| **Fuel** | lumber camp → smelter (charcoal) | bakery, kitchen, smelter | bread and iron both slow at once |
| **Metal** | mine → smelter | smithy (tools), armoury (armour) | tools wear out and guards stay in leather |

Three pools, six chains, and every chain touches at least two of them. That is
the interlock.

## What "maximising" a flow means here

Not more buildings. For each of the six chosen chains, the flow is finished
when all four of these are true:

1. **It works at step one.** The first building alone produces something
   useful (D-007).
2. **Every later step multiplies rather than unlocks.** Adding the mill makes
   the same grain go further; it never makes bread possible.
3. **It touches at least one shared pool**, so growing it costs something
   somewhere else.
4. **It has a visible bottleneck** the player can find without a wiki — a
   queue at the mill, an empty charcoal shelf, a smith idle for want of iron.
   The Tingbok names it; nobody has to guess.

Point 4 is the one most easily skipped and the one that decides whether the
system is *satisfying* or merely correct. A bottleneck the player cannot see is
not a decision, it is a mystery.

## Build order for the flows

Each is a slice, and each ends with the same evidence bar as every other slice
here: `full` green twice at one fingerprint, plus live video of the thing
happening.

1. **Food**, end to end — farmhouse → mill → bakery → kitchen → dining hall.
   The longest chain, the one everything else is paid for in, and the one that
   proves the worker/request machinery.
2. **Tools** — lumber → sawmill → carpenter, mine → smelter, meeting at the
   smithy. Introduces the two-input join and the fuel pool.
3. **Weapons & armour** — reuses the smelter, adds the contest in join 1.
4. **Drink & recruiting** — closes the population loop.
5. **Stone & repair** — gives raid damage a chain to be repaired by.
6. **Knowledge & care** — the services that make the rest survivable.
